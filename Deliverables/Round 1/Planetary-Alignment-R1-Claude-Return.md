## 2. Three priority claims or open questions

### Claim A. The evidence that trees transfer resources to kin through fungal networks is thinner than cited; the prevailing framing may also *under-recognize fungi*.

- **Source/location:** Karst, Jones & Hoeksema 2023, abstract; Henriksson et al. 2023, abstract.
- **Evidence type:** Observation (literature audit of citation patterns) + interpretation.
- **Content:** Karst et al. found claims of widespread networks and seedling-benefiting transfer insufficiently supported, and no peer-reviewed evidence of preferential transfer to offspring. Henriksson et al. read apparent sharing as tree–fungus trading.
- **Boundary and timescale:** Forest ectomycorrhizal systems. Isotope pulses run days to months; regeneration runs decades.
- **Alternatives:** Soil or root-exudate pathways; carbon retained in fungal tissue; fungal allocation for fungal benefit.
- **What would decide it:** Well-powered field experiments with hyphal-severing or mesh controls that track label into recipient *shoot* tissue across seasons. Short pulses measured only in roots leave it unresolved.
- **Status:** "No published evidence" means *not detected*, not *absent*.
- **Recognition point:** "Mother tree" language gives the agency to plants, and Henriksson's alternative moves it to the fungus. Debunking one attribution can surface another.

### Claim B. Learning and memory tests in plants and fungi hinge on whether the assay fits the organism, and that fit is itself disputed.

- **Source/location:** Gagliano et al. 2016; Markel 2020, abstract; Gagliano et al. 2020 comment, abstract; Markel 2020 reply, abstract; Fukasawa, Savoury & Boddy 2020, abstract.
- **Evidence type:** Observation (experiments, including a blinded replication) + conceptual argument about test validity.
- **Content (pea):** Markel's blinded replication failed. Gagliano et al. replied that his light stimulus did not reliably elicit phototropism, so conditioning was untestable. Markel replied that the setup differences cannot explain the failure, and did not claim the capacity is absent.
- **Content (fungus):** Mycelia moved to fresh soil regrew preferentially from the side that had faced a new resource. The authors read this as memory of growth direction.
- **Boundary and timescale:** Pea: 3-day training, single-generation seedlings. Fungus: weeks, soil microcosms, one species.
- **Alternatives:** Asymmetric residual structure such as cord biomass. That mechanism may *implement* memory rather than compete with it. The question is whether "memory" predicts anything beyond stored structure.
- **What would decide it:**
  - Pea: preregistered multi-lab replication with a validated unconditioned response.
  - Fungus: dissociating structural asymmetry from regrowth bias.
- **Status:** Pea learning is unresolved; there is evidence against it under Markel's conditions. Fungal memory has one-species support.

### Claim C. Fungal electrical activity is real; whether it is communication is currently non-identifiable with published methods.

- **Source/location:** Adamatzky 2022, abstract; Buffi et al. 2025, abstract and recording-methods section excerpt.
- **Evidence type:** Observation (spiking) + interpretation ("language") + complexity analysis.
- **Content:** Adamatzky grouped spikes into "words" whose length distributions resemble human languages. Buffi et al. describe fungal currents as low-magnitude and of unclear function.
- **Information measure:**
  - Partition: analyst-chosen spike threshold and inter-spike gap define a "word."
  - Measure: algorithmic and Lempel–Ziv complexity.
  - Baseline needed: shuffled or surrogate trains; killed-mycelium and substrate-only electrodes.
  - Confounds: moisture, electrode drift, growth.
  - My argument, not a finding: many stochastic processes yield language-like length distributions, so the resemblance is weak evidence.
- **What would decide it:** A signal at one site that predictably changes behaviour at another, with blocking controls.
- **Status:** Communication is non-identifiable, not refuted.

## 3. Disagreement map

| Axis | Positions | Nature | Bias risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plant cognition | Calvo et al. 2020: intelligence as adaptive, goal-directed behaviour, no brain required. Taiz et al. 2019: consciousness in plants effectively nil given the absence of brain structures. | Partly conceptual (definitions), partly empirical | Taiz's criterion takes animal brain architecture as threshold. That risks under-recognition by definition, though it is defensible *for consciousness* specifically. Calvo's broad definition risks over-attribution through equivocation. |
| CMN function | Simard tradition vs Karst/Henriksson | Empirical; testable | Positive citation bias is documented. Negative-result publication rates are unknown. |
| Gaia | Lovelock & Margulis 1974 (biosphere regulates *for* itself) vs Kirchner 2002 (biotic feedbacks not intrinsically homeostatic; may amplify warming) | Sign of feedbacks is empirical. "For" is teleological and conceptual. | Planetary-scale organism language imports purpose. Kirchner's critique can obscure genuine regulation. |
| Standing | Capacity-based (standing needs sentience or cognition) vs relational (standing from kinship, dependence, intrinsic value) | Ethical and conceptual; not settled by data | Tying standing to intelligence makes every null result an ethical verdict. |

**Does ethical concern depend on demonstrated intelligence?** Not necessarily. Te Awa Tupua grounds the river's legal personhood in intrinsic values and Whanganui iwi relationship, not in cognition. The costs: the grounds must be argued rather than detected, and the question of who speaks remains.

**Representation and consent.** Te Pou Tupua is two appointed humans, one nominated by the iwi and one by the Crown, who act in the river's name and interests (ss 18–20). That is a *proposed way to recognize* standing, not evidence that the river authorized anything. It belongs to a specific Treaty settlement and is not a portable template.

**CARE** governs Indigenous Peoples' rights in *their* data and knowledges. It gives researchers, and me, no authority to speak for fungi, rivers or "the planet." Drawing on Indigenous ontologies here without Indigenous governance is itself an extraction risk.

**Harms:**
- Over-attribution: the network narrative has reached forest-management discourse (Karst et al.).
- Under-recognition: nulls read as absence; fungal interests are eclipsed by plant-centred framing.
- Material cost: the IEA base case has data-centre electricity roughly doubling to ~945 TWh by 2030. This report draws on that load, at a marginal footprint I cannot quantify.

**Counterarguments to my framing** (scale-matched functional definitions; *not detected* kept distinct from *absent*):

1. *Deflationary:* Calling regrowth "memory" may be equivocation that yields no new predictions, so parsimony favours physiological description (Taiz).
2. *Relational/Indigenous:* My framing still makes Western empirical detection the gatekeeper. Kin obligations do not wait on assays, and treating Te Awa Tupua and CARE as "cases" repeats the extraction I warn against.

## 4. Ontology and boundary note

**Assumed:** Mechanistic–process pluralism. Capacities are functional profiles of processes, and mechanisms *implement* them. A mechanism dissolves a capacity only if the capacity term adds no predictive content. Individuals are boundaries chosen per question.
- *Reveals:* testable definitions and clean null/negative distinctions.
- *Obscures:* obligations not framed as capacities; slow or diffuse processes outside lab timescales.

**Alternative:** Relational/holobiont. Gilbert, Sapp & Tauber 2012 argue that anatomical, genetic and immunological individuality break down under symbiosis, so the unit is the consortium.
- *Reveals:* "does the tree learn?" may target the wrong unit.
- *Obscures:* conflicts of interest *within* the consortium, such as Henriksson's trading model.

**The actual conflict** is over the unit of analysis, not mechanism versus relation.
- **Partly testable:** whether fitness, integration and co-transmission boundaries match holobiont boundaries (partner-fidelity and selection data).
- **Needs conceptual argument:** whether relations are ontologically prior to relata.

## 5. Next bounded desk-research step

**Question:** Among published field and greenhouse studies of inter-plant carbon transfer via mycorrhizal networks, how many:
- included a hyphal-severing or mesh control,
- tracked label into recipient shoot tissue, and
- reported effect sizes?

And do reported effects differ by control type?

**Needs:** full texts of the studies Karst et al. evaluated plus newer ones; a preregistered coding sheet separating *not detected*, *evidence against* and *not tested*; two independent coders with disagreements logged.

**Limit:** This cannot settle plant or fungal agency. It can show whether the existing record is informative at all.

**Dissent retained:** Claims A–C do not converge on one ontology, and cross-model agreement on them would not be independent confirmation.
