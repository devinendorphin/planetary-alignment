## 2. Three priority claims or open questions

**Claim A — Trees transfer carbon and defence signals to neighbours and kin through common mycorrhizal networks.** Sources: Simard et al. 1997 (field isotopes); Gorzelak et al. 2015 (review, Table 1); Song et al. 2015 (defence enzymes). Evidence: isotope tracing and enzyme assays, mostly potted seedlings, single seasons, interior Douglas-fir; ~24 h signal timescales. Alternatives: soil diffusion, fungal control of transfer serving fungal fitness, root contact. Strengthened by: replication with the signal molecule identified. Weakened by: well-powered field hyphal-exclusion at stand scale. Left unresolved: mesh results like Pickles 2017, where label reportedly crossed hyphae-blocking barriers — if verified, evidence against CMN-specificity under those conditions, not against transfer itself.

**Claim B — Plants learn by association.** Source: Gagliano et al. 2016, single-lab Y-maze conditioning; cultivar Massey Gem, small n, days-long timescales; the replication's ~40% attrition shows the assay's sensitivity to growth-rate variance. The well-powered, blinded replication (Markel 2020) failed, including on the control phototropic response the original design assumes — an unsuitable-test problem cutting both ways. Unresolved, not refuted: independent preregistered replication with original materials could settle the narrow claim.

**Claim C — The CMN literature shows positive citation bias.** Source: Karst et al. 2023: 18 "influential" studies, citations coded supported/unsupported; an Author Correction exists. Alternatives per Simard et al. 2025: subjective study selection, sample inflated to ~273 by counting years since publication, 28% self-coauthored coded studies. Bibliometrically testable, but neither side's coding is transparent enough to settle it. The skeptical cascade deserves equal scrutiny: Robinson et al. cite Karst and Henriksson for load-bearing claims while sharing authors with them — a mirrored, smaller cascade.

## 3. Disagreement map

*Empirical.* (i) Does transferred carbon enter receiver shoots in physiologically meaningful amounts? Defenders say yes; critics say trivial or extraradical. (ii) Do mesh/trenching controls exclude soil pathways? Critics say no; defenders note the 1997 paper reported both hyphal and soil pathways. (iii) Is the "mother tree" a hypothesis or a memoir metaphor? Robinson/Henriksson treat it as a hypothesis with eight cited proponents; Simard et al. say those eight articles never state it — a checkable attribution question. (iv) Detection asymmetry: isotopes detect atom movement, not "communication"; enzyme assays detect response, not intent; negative results (Markel 2020) are as scale-bound as positive ones.

*Conceptual.* Over-attribution risk: bestsellers and press outrun peer review, and both camps slip from "transfer" to "sharing." Under-recognition risk: fungal agency and soil pathways are flattened into "pipeline" talk on both sides; Indigenous accounts of elder trees appear as rhetorical citations rather than knowledge with conditions of use under CARE. Indexing asymmetry: critiques appear in high-impact reviews, primary CMN work in specialty journals — citation counts then measure venue, not truth.

## 4. Ontology and boundary note

**Assumed:** a relational/process ontology — sensing, signalling, plasticity, and distributed memory are real where operationally detectable, with no brains, bounded bodies, or human-like minds as admission criteria; the unit of individuality (plant, fungus, holobiont, network) stays open. **Alternative compared:** mechanistic individualism — selection and control reside in organisms; CMN effects are fungal brokerage or byproducts; "communication" requires representation, so chemical exchange is not communication. These are not wholly incompatible: both accept small-scale transfer. The actual conflict is whether agency-language adds causal content. Testable now: transfer magnitudes, signal identity, replication. Not testable by these means: whether any experience-like interior exists (conceptual argument), and which explanatory unit fits best (comparative model fit). The relational view reveals network-scale capacities that individualist protocols miss; the individualist view exposes attribution errors the relational view smooths over. Each is a detection instrument with blind spots.
